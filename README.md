<p>The &lsquo;Airtel Thanks&rsquo; app allows customers to recharge their Airtel Payments Bank Rupay NCMC cards on-the-go. The payment interface is facing a lot of customer dissatisfaction with overwhelming features in terms of UI and UX, causing rising drop-offs. The goal is to reduce information overload and provide smooth user onboarding, offering an efficient transaction experience without compromising on ads.</p>
<h2>Project Overview</h2>
<p>To redesign the Airtel Payments Bank NCMC recharge flow by reducing the number of screens and updating the UI to make the buttons stand out. New additions involve adding a micro-recharge option of ₹50 and adding a payment process status bar for users to follow to complete the transaction.</p>
<h2>Problem</h2>
<p>The app allows users to perform all tasks related to their RuPay NCMC card. However, due to the poor UI on the home screen and information overload due to too many screens causing freezing, user frustration leads to app abandonment.</p>
<h2>Objectives</h2>
<h3><strong>UI &amp; UX Retouch</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Improve the UI of the app for quick and efficient navigation.</li>
<li>Reduce cognitive load by reducing the number of screens required</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Feature Addition</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Micro-recharge option for quick selection recharge.</li>
<li>Payment status and progress bar on top of screen to reduce drop-off.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Constraints</h2>
<ol>
<li>For recharges done partially underground or in low-connectivity zones, the app must not freeze completely. The payment processing must be visible, or any discrepancy must be communicated via visuals.</li>
<li>The engineering team must focus on immediate local caching of the app to ensure smooth transaction processing without the user being aware of it.</li>
<li>There is a time and testing constraint with the feature rolling out as soon as possible with a minimal GTM budget.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Persona</h2>
<ol>
<li>The College Day Scholar (18-24 yrs)</li>
<li>A Working Single Mother (40-55 yrs)</li>
<li>Senior Citizen on Foot (60-70 yrs)</li>
</ol>
<h2>Use Cases</h2>
<h3><strong>Scenario 1: Rohan, The College Day Scholar</strong></h3>
<p>Rohan, a 20-year-old college scholar in Delhi, needs to quickly top-up his NCMC metro card on his smartphone while walking to the turnstiles. He relies on UPI micro-recharges (₹50&ndash;₹100) to avoid long queues at metro ticket booths and AVMs. However, he currently faces major friction, including slow screen loads due to poor underground connectivity, excessive navigation steps, and a lack of payment progress indicators.</p>
<h3><strong>Scenario 2: Anitha, A Working Single Mother</strong></h3>
<p>Anitha Pillai, a 44-year-old single mother and operations manager in Gurugram, recharges her and her son's NCMC transit cards on payday using a fixed monthly budget of ₹1000 or above. Because she performs huge transactions, she gets anxious when payments process without immediate status feedback. The current app journey introduces friction through excessive selection screens, lack of real-time transaction indicators, and delayed balance updates post-travel.</p>
<h3><strong>Scenario 3: Ramesh, Senior Citizen on Foot</strong></h3>
<p>Ramesh Chandra, a 66-year-old retired government employee from Greater Noida, wants to use his NCMC card to travel across the city to visit relatives without worrying about recharges. Due to low-to-moderate tech proficiency and vision issues, he frequently relies on his children or grandchildren to recharge his card online before he leaves home. He struggles with small text sizes when typing card details, complex jargons, and paranoia over potential payment fraud or accidental double-deductions. When travelling alone without help, his anxiety over app errors often forces him to revert to buying physical station tokens or QR tickets with cash.</p>
<h2>Assumption &amp; Biases</h2>
<h3><strong>Assumptions</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The user has onboarded the card details and is exploring features on the app.</li>
<li>The user is at least primary-grade educated and has/needs no impairment assistance.</li>
<li>The user is on the go and is in a hurry to recharge the smart card using UPI.</li>
<li>The user does not own an Airtel Payments Bank account.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Biases</strong></h3>
<p>Competitors provide their NCMC card payment services on their dedicated mobile apps as well as on UPI providers like Paytm, Google Pay, PhonePe, etc. The aim is to retain the user on the Airtel Thanks app by avoiding user drop-off mid-payment to other apps.</p>
